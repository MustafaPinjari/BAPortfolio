The boundary between pure business analysis and software engineering is closing rapidly. In modern product teams, the **Technical Business Analyst (Tech BA)** plays an essential role: understanding not just *what* the business needs, but *how* data moves across APIs, microservices, and databases.

## 1. Why APIs Matter in Business Analysis

When a business process involves multi-system integration (such as processing an e-commerce payment or registering a student), the BA must map the data contract.

Key questions a Technical BA investigates:
- What payload attributes are required vs. optional?
- What happens when a third-party service fails (timeout, 5xx server error)?
- Are sensitive fields (tokens, PII) masked or encrypted at rest?

## 2. Maintaining an Actionable Data Dictionary

A Data Dictionary prevents cross-functional confusion by defining business terms alongside database column constraints:

| Entity Field | Data Type | Constraint | Business Definition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `order_id` | `VARCHAR(32)` | Unique, Not Null | Unique identifier assigned to customer order. |
| `tax_amount` | `DECIMAL(10,2)` | Non-Negative | Cumulative GST amount calculated at checkout. |
| `is_quarantined` | `BOOLEAN` | Default: `FALSE` | Soft flag indicating whether a file is archived. |

---

## 3. As-Is vs. To-Be Workflow Mapping

Before recommending any technical automation, a BA must document the current operational reality (**As-Is**) using flowcharts or BPMN swimlanes. Identifying friction points—such as manual spreadsheet reconciliations or disconnected email approvals—allows the BA to design an optimized future state (**To-Be**) with measurable ROI.

## 4. Summary

Technical depth does not mean writing production code every day—it means speaking the language of developers, asking the right questions about data architecture, and designing specifications that withstand real-world edge cases.
