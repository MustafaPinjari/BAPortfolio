The biggest risk in software delivery isn't flawed code—it's building the wrong system with total technical precision. 

Elicitation is the art of uncovering unspoken assumptions, regulatory constraints, and operational bottlenecks that stakeholders rarely articulate in formal meetings.

## 1. The 5 Whys & Root Cause Elicitation

Stakeholders almost universally present **symptoms** as requirements:
- *Stakeholder*: "We need a faster export button on the invoice grid."
- *BA (Why 1)*: "Why do users need to export the grid daily?" → "To cross-check payment statuses."
- *BA (Why 2)*: "Why aren't payment statuses visible inside the platform?" → "Because reconciliation happens in another tool."
- *BA (Why 3)*: "Why isn't that tool integrated?" → "We never automated the webhook."

**True Requirement**: Real-time payment webhook integration, rendering manual CSV exports completely obsolete.

## 2. Managing Stakeholder Conflict via RACI Matrices

When marketing, compliance, and engineering disagree on feature behavior, an explicit **RACI** matrix restores momentum:

- **Responsible (R)**: Who builds and executes the feature.
- **Accountable (A)**: The single business owner with final approval authority.
- **Consulted (C)**: Subject matter experts providing input (Legal, InfoSec, UX).
- **Informed (I)**: Stakeholders notified of milestone progress.

## 3. Collaborative Discovery Workshops

Instead of endless email threads, organize time-boxed **Event Storming** and **User Story Mapping** sessions with cross-functional participants to align on product priorities in a single afternoon.
